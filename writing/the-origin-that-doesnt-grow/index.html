Your origin doesn't get busier as your audience grows to millions

Centralized live video sends every viewer their own copy from a server you pay for, so cost tracks the audience exactly. Herald's origin doesn't: at one hundred thousand viewers, it still sends eight copies of each segment.

September 2026

Twitch and YouTube send live video from a server farm sized to the audience, because every viewer gets their own copy of every second. Grow from a thousand viewers to two million and you need two thousand times the bandwidth, because the origin is the only thing sending.

Herald's viewers send to each other instead. The video below grows one from a handful of viewers to two million and shows exactly what the origin sends the whole time.

One stream, growing from a handful of viewers to two million.

What the origin actually sends

At one hundred thousand viewers pulling a stream, the origin sends eight copies of each segment: sixteen megabits a second. Not one hundred thousand copies. Not a thousand.

Below about four thousand viewers, that number climbs on its own. A hundred viewers costs the origin 1.19 sends a segment, a thousand costs 3.61, because a viewer that hears about a new segment from another viewer waits three seconds for the swarm to deliver it before asking the origin at all. Past four thousand viewers, a ceiling takes over: eight sends a segment, and no more, however much larger the crowd gets.

Carry the stream out to two million viewers and the origin still sends exactly eight copies a segment, the same sixteen megabits it was sending at four thousand. The one-copy-per-viewer version Twitch runs would need four terabits a second at that size. About two hundred and fifty thousand times more.

What sets it apart

Every live platform at real scale hits the same wall: one server can't send a copy to every viewer once the audience gets big, so you either build a CDN sized to your peak or find another way to move the bytes. Herald's bet is that the audience already is the delivery network, and past a few thousand viewers its origin's bill stops moving at all: sixteen megabits, whether four thousand people are watching or two million.
