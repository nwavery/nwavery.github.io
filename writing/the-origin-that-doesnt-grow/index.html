Your origin server doesn't get busier as your audience grows to millions

Centralized live video sends every viewer their own copy from a server you pay for, so cost tracks the audience exactly. Herald's origin server doesn't: at 100,000 viewers, it still sends eight copies of each segment.

September 2026

Twitch and YouTube send live video from a server farm sized to the audience, because every viewer gets their own copy of every second. Grow from 1,000 viewers to 2,000,000 and you need 2,000 times the bandwidth, because the origin server is the only thing sending.

Herald's viewers send to each other instead. The video below grows one from a handful of viewers to 2,000,000 and shows exactly what the origin sends the whole time.

One stream, growing from a handful of viewers to 2,000,000.

What the origin actually sends

At 100,000 viewers pulling a stream, the origin sends eight copies of each segment, a few seconds of video: 16 megabits a second. Not 100,000 copies. Not 1,000.

Below about 4,000 viewers, that number climbs on its own. 100 viewers costs the origin 1.19 sends per segment, 1,000 costs 3.61, because a viewer that hears about a new segment from another viewer waits three seconds for the other viewers to deliver it before asking the origin at all. Past 4,000 viewers, a ceiling takes over: eight sends per segment, and no more, however much larger the crowd gets.

Carry the stream out to 2,000,000 viewers and the origin still sends exactly eight copies per segment, the same 16 megabits it was sending at 4,000. The one-copy-per-viewer version Twitch runs would need 4 terabits a second at that size. About 250,000 times more.

What sets it apart

Every live platform at real scale hits the same wall: one server can't send a copy to every viewer once the audience gets big, so you either build a server network, a CDN, sized to your peak or find another way to move the bytes. Herald's bet is that the audience already is the delivery network: past a few thousand viewers, the origin's bill stops moving at all, so growing from a few thousand viewers to millions costs nothing extra in servers.
